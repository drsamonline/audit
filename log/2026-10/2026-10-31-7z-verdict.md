# 2026-10-31 — Does 7z earn its place? Yes. And the conversion pass would have been wrong.

## The measurement

Every store, logical tree size (what the files add up to) against pack size (what the
repository actually occupies on disk):

    store      tree     pack    ratio
    dumps     1290.0M   748.1M    58%
    dumps-2    939.0M   354.7M    38%
    dumps-3    712.0M   545.4M    77%
    dumps-4    839.0M   653.2M    78%
    dumps-5    206.0M   196.5M    95%
    TOTAL     3986.0M  2497.9M    63%

## What it says

**7z earns its place.** `dumps-5` is the only store built entirely with 7z, and its pack is
95% of its tree - essentially no waste. Its 206 MB of tree is what 7z produced; at the 18%
gzip penalty measured on the same data it would be about 250 MB. In a fresh store the pack
tracks the tree, so that 18% is real disk, not just a smaller listing.

**Dedupe's saving is visible here too, and it is not disk.** The older stores pack to 38-78%
of their tree because git stores identical blobs once. That is why removing 600 duplicate
snapshots moved the pack not at all: they were already shared. Dedupe buys a smaller tree -
faster clones, readable history - not bytes.

## The conversion pass I suggested last turn would have made things worse

Re-compressing existing gzip dumps to 7z commits NEW blobs. Git keeps the old ones in
history forever. The pack would have GROWN by the size of every 7z blob written, while the
gzip originals stayed. I proposed it, measured it, and it is the wrong move - so it is not
being built.

The only thing that shrinks an existing store is rewriting its history: an orphan branch
holding just the current snapshots, force-pushed. That is destructive, it drops the record
of what was deleted, and it is the owner's decision - not something to do because a number
looks big.

## Verdict

    7z:      KEEP. 18-28% smaller snapshots, and in a fresh store that is disk.
    dedupe:  KEEP. Smaller tree, faster sweeps, readable history. Not a disk saving.
    convert: DO NOT BUILD. It would grow the pack.
    compact: the only real disk win left, and it needs an explicit decision.
